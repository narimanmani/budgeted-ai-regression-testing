Archive validation is recorded separately after this report is sealed.**

## Fixed main comparison

The design has two applications, two repetitions, two pair-attempt ceilings (K2/K4),
cold/acquired history and five methods: cost-only, impact-only, history-only,
always-combined and CG1. Each trajectory executes fault A, its repair and fault B.
Complete trajectory order is mirrored in repetition2. The application/repetition
order is OpenWebUI1, Dify1, OpenWebUI2, Dify2.

Each application/repetition block contains 20 trajectories and at most 568
native executions: 48 common old-state profile queries, 160 own warm-history
queries, and 360 selected old/new executions. The four-block ceiling is 2,272.
Every warm trajectory physically acquires its own eight old/new pairs. Reference
outcomes and other trajectories' histories cannot become its operational history.

That warm acquisition executes HEALTHY to A_BAD on the same eight tasks before
the scored A introduction. It is a paid warm-start condition with prior exposure
to that fault and workload, rather than prediction of a previously unobserved
A change. The later B introduction tests transfer of that acquired history to
another fault family. Cold trajectories begin without the acquisition. A benefit
at the first warm A episode cannot by itself establish generalization to a new
regression or cost-effective history acquisition.

Paid old profiles use three native executions per task. Matched methods share
identical estimated costs and observed old contexts. A_BAD profiles are released
only after all twenty episode1 trajectories close. Native state preparation is
paid common work before individual policy timers; at most two APIs are resident.
Each episode has a pair ceiling and the prospectively fixed wall safeguard
`ceil(10 + 1.5*K*max(old_pair_cost_estimates))`.

Module-level change evidence is broad in these TXT pipelines and can admit all
eight tasks. This study does not establish selective-impact benefit in such
conditions. A simpler selected mechanism label alone is not measured savings.
The frozen utility criterion requires a strict matched detection gain, or equal
detection with fewer actual native queries including acquisition. All fixed
arms remain comparators, reporting every fixed arm whether a simpler strategy,
the combined strategy or CG1 performs best.

All methods have already acquired the common old profiles and context evidence
before CG1 chooses a mechanism. Warm acquisition is also fixed by the assigned
regime and is paid even by methods that ignore its history. The experiment
therefore evaluates decisions given that evidence, rather than an adaptive
decision about whether to purchase evidence or history. Disabling a mechanism
does not recover its already paid acquisition cost. When impact admits every
task, its toggle can also yield no distinct selection treatment. These facts
limit interpretation of the method contrast even if the primary criteria pass.

Warm and cold are historical-evidence regimes, not cold/warm process caches.
Cold means initially empty history; both regimes subsequently retain their own
selected observations, which history-enabled methods may use at later changes.
Warm acquisition costs are retained, including experimental acquisitions for
arms that subsequently ignore history. Comparisons between regimes do not by
themselves establish that acquiring history repays its cost relative to spending
the same extra resources on other tests. Native index reuse, embedding caches,
usage counters and fresh conversation identities are explicitly recorded.

The online pair ceiling and the whole-trajectory resource cost answer different
questions. A complete cold trajectory allocates 48 profiling queries plus 12
selected queries at K2, or 24 at K4: 60 or 72 queries in total. A warm trajectory
also acquires 16 own-history queries, giving 76 or 88. These are component
allocations, not additional physical queries to add across matched methods.
Executing all eight old/new pairs at each of three changes would instead
require 48 queries without profiling or history acquisition. That arithmetic
does not establish its observed detection or runtime, and full retesting exceeds
the K2/K4 online ceiling. A separately declared secondary full-suite diagnostic
was locked after the primary execution began and before its own queries, then
executed in full. It leaves the primary design and success criteria unchanged
and cannot repair a failed primary result.

### Concrete helpful and harmful ordering traces

In Dify repetition1, K2 warm history, CG1's episode3 gate rejects history because
the verified B change path does not overlap the earlier A events. Its recorded
pre-dispatch prefix is `VDF08, VDF04`; VDF04 exposes B. Always-combined instead
selects `VDF02, VDF01`, both earlier A targets, and misses that B opportunity.
This accounts for the single additional K2 detection.

In OpenWebUI repetition2, K2 warm history, the initial own positive counts are
one each for VOW01, VOW02 and the reference-discordant VOW07 format failure.
The cost tie-break gives CG1 the prefix `VOW07, VOW01`, leaving the original
A target VOW02 untested in that episode. After feedback, VOW07 and VOW01 each
have count2, while VOW02 has count1. The A and B changes share the coarse module
token, so episode3 still accepts history and selects `VOW07, VOW01`, missing
both B-sensitive tests. The corresponding cost-only prefix is `VOW08, VOW03`,
which exposes B through VOW03. The VOW07 observation is a real violation of the
literal contract, not demonstrated erroneous feedback; the limit is retaining
an earlier observed failure for a later change on the strength of coarse path
overlap. These are post hoc explanations of recorded decisions, not new rules
or selected replacements for the full result table.

## Operational/reference disagreement in OpenWebUI

There are 26 selected-pair transition disagreements across the 720 selected
pairs. They all reduce to one task/state in OpenWebUI repetition2: VOW07 in
A_BAD. Its correct literal is `TERRACE-703`; all 39 observed responses in that
native index instead contain `[TERRACE-703]`. These observations comprise three
paid OLD profiles, ten own warm acquisitions, six episode1 selected queries and
twenty episode2 selected queries. The separately executed A_BAD reference index
passed 2/2, and main repetition1 passed 13/13. Healthy and B_BAD observations of
this task remain unbracketed and correct.

The six episode1 alarm occurrences span the three history-enabled methods,
giving two reference-discordant alarms per method. The twenty episode2 pairs
are observed repairs where the reference bank labels preserved correctness.
These are **not established oracle false positives**: the actual bracketed
answer violates the unchanged bare-label contract, and both checker
implementations agree. The source-mechanism control partition includes VOW07,
but the bad-state admission controls were specifically VOW05/06.

The read-only forensic audit checked 124 generations. Original target bytes,
native parsed text, all eight retrieved texts and their order, non-message
generation parameters and authorized backend provenance agree across the
reference and both main instances. The actual model messages become identical
after replacing only the native collection resource-ID UUID. The correct label
reaches generation in every instance. Thus the recorded difference concerns
reproducible format noncompliance in a different native prompt/index instance,
not a missing fact or checker implementation error. A causal UUID effect has
not been established; no counterfactual native query was added.

The freeze fixes source documents, questions, configuration and code. Native
index-generated resource IDs are recorded runtime outputs; they were not forced
to be identical between independently built indexes. This difference does not
change the frozen public cases, but it changes the complete messages seen by
the model and limits a claim that generation inputs were byte-identical.

A stable reference bank within one index therefore did not guarantee identical
contract outcomes after rebuilding the native index. The original reference
frame and all primary metrics remain unchanged.

## Conclusion and remaining scope

CG1 meets its finite-frame criteria with a small Dify warm-history gain at equal
allocated query demand. OpenWebUI supplies a history-transfer limit and an
additional native-instance format failure that a separate reference bank did
not capture. Most repeated fault opportunities remain undetected. These results
support a bounded account of using acquired history under pair ceilings, not
general validated mechanism-selection guidance or the full proposed
impact-evidence--judgment--history interaction contribution.

The engineering implication within these cases is conditional. When a small
catalog can be fully retested without the hard online pair ceiling, full paired
retesting is the stronger observed baseline and avoids fresh profiling/history
query overhead. When K2/K4 is binding and evidence has already been acquired,
history gating can recover some detections, but coarse shared module tokens
still retain unhelpful history in OpenWebUI. Reference banks also need an
explicit scope over native prompt/index instances. These findings do not
establish a universal technique map, an adaptive evidence-purchase policy,
alternative-oracle effects or portability to untouched application settings.

The validation workload changes record identities and labels while retaining
the development task format and fault families. It tests transfer within that
controlled workload class. The two native implementations are independently
developed upstream applications; the workloads, interventions and evaluations
are authored by this study. They are not independent industrial case studies.
The primary checker remains a fixed factual contract throughout, so these
experiments do not estimate the effect of changing failure judgments or of
semantic or human oracles. Two execution repetitions also do not constitute
independent application or defect samples. The full proposed contribution needs
broader evidence than successful enrollment or complete execution alone.
