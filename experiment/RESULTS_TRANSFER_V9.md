# Frozen-guidance transfer, mechanism interactions and complete request costs

**Completed follow-up study, 1 November 2026.** Two upstream implementations not
used to develop CG1, four independently sourced historical fixes, and all registered
native observations were executed. The new evidence supports conditional benefits
and operating limits. It does not establish that CG1 is generally preferable or
that guidance transfers to complete, independently deployed applications.

The clearest fresh result favors simple retesting: under the same 28-request
whole-test ceiling, full paired retesting observed two regression-revealing task
pairs per setting using 16 requests. CG1 used 28, including 24 profiling requests,
and observed zero in Vanna and one in Rasa. Longer-sequence replay shows that
selective mechanisms can recover their acquisition cost when exact states recur,
and that judgment errors change how impact and history interact.

## Budget and replay semantics

The frozen bank supports seven strategies: seeded random order, profiled latency
order (`cost_only`), impact plus latency, history plus latency, always-combined,
CG1 and budgeted catalog-order retesting. The last strategy executes the entire
catalog whenever its allowance permits it.

The primary comparison has two constructed 16-change orders, observed edges
versus seeded omission with probability 0.25, exact judgments versus seeded
verdict flips with probability 0.25,
128/256 total native-request ceilings and three recorded passes: **672
counterfactual trajectories**. Predeclared 4/8-change prefixes add 1,344 secondary
trajectories. These 2,016 trajectories and 18,816 decisions are recorded-outcome
replay, not new native executions or independent defect samples.

Every selected old/new pair costs two request units. Methods consuming profiles
pay three old requests for each of eight tasks in each distinct old state: 24
requests per state, at most 72 for H/A/B. Only exact-state profiles can be reused.
Random and full retesting buy no profiles. History contains only the strategy's
earlier selected operational pairs; there is no free warm-history bank. Profile
costs are not refunded when CG1 disables a mechanism. A uniform
remaining-episode allocator applies to all methods.

**The matched resource is a native-request ceiling, not elapsed time.** Measured
latency orders the profiled strategies, but cheaper individual queries cannot
buy additional request capacity under this allocator. Consequently, this is
not a demonstration of wall-budget optimization or production cost optimality.
Recorded native, setup, checker and feature wall components, controller CPU and
the explicitly labeled wall-plus-CPU hybrid proxy are reported separately.
Controller wall time and energy are not measured. Timer zeros do not establish
free computation. Research engineering, screening and artifact-production time
are outside the strategy operating-cost comparison.

## Fresh, physically executed policy comparison

Both methods receive 28 requests for one first-A change, with empty history and
exact operational contracts. CG1 physically acquires 24 old profile queries,
then spends its remaining four requests on two fresh pairs. Full retesting
executes eight fresh pairs for 16 requests; there is no artificial K2 restriction
preventing it from using its allowance. Each decision hash was bound into the
ledger before the selected endpoints were dispatched.

| Setting | Method | Profiling requests | Selected endpoint requests | Total requests | Observed regression-revealing pairs |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Vanna | CG1 | 24 | 4 | 28 | 0 |
| Vanna | Full paired retesting | 0 | 16 | 16 | 2 |
| Rasa | CG1 | 24 | 4 | 28 | 1 |
| Rasa | Full paired retesting | 0 | 16 | 16 | 2 |

CG1 selected Vanna's unaffected `VC01, VC02`, and Rasa's affected `RF01` plus
control `RF03`. The table reports observations from each method's own fresh
pairs; it does not borrow the other method's outcomes as a live recall
denominator. Two revealing tasks in a setting express one A fault, not two
independent bugs. These four cells do not validate long history or noisy
judgments live. The observed native/checker/feature wall allocations were also
larger for CG1: 50.274 versus 36.451 seconds in Vanna, and 1.611 versus 1.102
seconds in Rasa. Fixed execution order and shared prepared platforms prevent a
causal deployment-speedup interpretation.

## Primary replay results, including the unfavorable comparisons

The following counts summarize **32 matched condition strata**, each aggregating
three recorded passes. Strata and task opportunities are not independent bugs.

| Comparator | CG1 detection gains | Ties | Losses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Full/budgeted catalog retesting | 0 | 17 | 15 |
| Seeded random | 2 | 15 | 15 |
| Profiled latency order | 26 | 6 | 0 |
| Impact plus latency | 8 | 23 | 1 |
| History plus latency | 20 | 10 | 2 |
| Always-combined | 1 | 30 | 1 |

Against always-combined, the one gain is one repeated detection and the loss is
three, for a net difference of **two fewer** detections. Allocated request demand
is equal, and CG1 raises additional discordant alarms in three strata. Thus
simplifying mechanism labels did not establish request savings over always-combined or
reliable superiority. Improvements over a weak profiled latency baseline cannot
stand in for success against full retesting or random selection.

The full condition tables and case-level differences are retained in the
presentation CSVs, rather than selecting only favorable examples.

## Where acquisition pays off, and the limit of that finding

With observed edges, exact judgments, the 256-request ceiling and the 16-change
**interleaved** order, CG1 detects **48/48 repeated opportunities per setting**
across three recorded passes. Its standalone allocation is 200 requests per
trajectory in Rasa and 240 in Vanna, versus 256 for full retesting. This saves
56 requests (21.9%) and 16 requests (6.25%), respectively, at equal detection in
these cells. Impact-only and always-combined also attain complete detection;
this does not establish a unique CG1 advantage.

For the same balanced, exact, observed-edge condition at the high per-change
allowance, the prefix results are:

| Setting | Changes | CG1 repeated detections | CG1 requests per trajectory | Full repeated detections | Full requests |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rasa | 4 | 6/12 | 60 | 12/12 | 64 |
| Rasa | 8 | 21/24 | 128 | 24/24 | 128 |
| Rasa | 16 | 48/48 | 200 | 48/48 | 256 |
| Vanna | 4 | 6/12 | 62 | 12/12 | 64 |
| Vanna | 8 | 15/24 | 128 | 24/24 | 128 |
| Vanna | 16 | 48/48 | 240 | 48/48 | 256 |

**This crossover depends on revisiting the same H/A/B source states.** It is an
optimistic recurrence/cache-reuse scenario, not evidence that profiles amortize
over unseen, continuously evolving versions. Detection counts aggregate three
passes; request counts are per trajectory. Blocked prefixes are reported only
descriptively because their 4/8-change prefixes contain A alone, whereas their
16-change sequence contains A and B.

## Impact, judgments and history actually interact

The registered factors were exercised: selective admissions occurred, edge
omissions changed admissions, selected pairs generated own positive history,
history was sometimes enabled and deliberately corrupted operational judgments
altered feedback. The independent review recomputed all 96 fixed I×H contrasts
and 48 checker-moderation contrasts. They describe finite sequential regimes,
not population causal effects or natural checker error rates.

A concrete primary example is Vanna, blocked order, omitted edges and the
128-request ceiling. Aggregating three passes gives:

| Operational judgments | Latency order C | Impact I | History H | Combined IH | IH − I − H + C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Exact | 0 | 6 | 0 | 12 | +6 |
| Seeded flips (p = 0.25) | 0 | 2 | 0 | 5 | +3 |

These are detections among 48 repeated opportunities per row. Corrupting
judgments changes the interaction by −3. In that noisy cell CG1 detects only 2,
versus combined's 5, and raises 13 versus 11 discordant alarms.

Recorded decisions explain both directions of the guide's behavior. In a
blocked B episode, the global compatibility gate rejects history containing
another change scope, retains cheap control `VC02`, and misses a B target that
combined promotes as previously failing `VF02`. In a different interleaved SQL
episode, rejecting stale figure history admits both SQL targets into the CG1
prefix and contributes the single positive primary comparison. These are post
hoc explanations of precommitted decisions, not new rules fitted to these
validation outcomes. The global gate can discard useful local feedback as well
as prevent stale feedback from displacing current targets.

## Retained background failure

Vanna task `VS04` fails in all 21 validation observations checked: 12 primary,
four fourth-pass and five live/profile observations. The actual generated CTE
ends with `SELECT total_amount;` rather than selecting from its `totals` CTE.
SQLite rejects it. All corresponding completions stopped normally after 23
tokens, below the 256-token ceiling. The error is already present in the
healthy state and persists across changed states.

This is one background generated-SQL failure pattern, not 21 independent defects,
not another independently sourced upstream case and not a demonstrated checker
implementation error or output truncation. It remains in every analysis;
false-to-false pairs are not newly introduced regressions. The prescribed
witnesses for all four historical faults still expose their source changes.

## Conclusion and the journal claim

The requested missing conditions now have executable, audited evidence on four
new upstream mechanisms. Full acquisition accounting changes the conclusion:
simple full retesting wins the fresh small cold-start cases; selective mechanisms
can match detection with fewer requests in repeated cached states; and imperfect
judgments change the interaction of impact and acquired history. The frozen CG1
gate helps in one primary condition and harms in another.

The defensible contribution is **a bounded empirical characterization of these
interactions and operating limits on native AI application paths**. Broad,
externally validated guidance for complete independently deployed applications
remains unestablished. Four bugs, authored recurrence sequences, synthetic
omissions/errors and replay do not warrant a universal mechanism map or a TOSEM
readiness guarantee.

The next scientific step is a budget-aware selection design that decides whether
to acquire evidence at all, followed by prospective validation on new changes
and complete application workflows. A request-quota objective needs appropriate
uniform-cost baselines; a wall-budget claim needs actual wall-constrained
execution. These cases must become development/characterization evidence for any
revised rule, rather than being reused as its fresh validation. No additional
native runs were added to pursue a positive result.
